  1. 环球

    美国 10 月通胀高于预期，美股三大指数周二收低

    美国 10 月消费者物价指数按月涨 0.4%，核心物价涨 0.3%，均高于市场预期，10 年期美债收益率大涨 13.8 个基点至 5.00%，市场押注联储局 12 月升息。道指收跌 0.30% 至 52,231.89 点，标普 500 跌 0.38%，纳指跌 0.74%；辉达因总裁相关诉讼风波盘中挫约 6%。

    来源： TheStreet ↗
  2. IPO

    SKA Capital 今天登陆 ACE 市场，九个月净赚 RM26.1m

    变电站工程商 SKA Capital 以每股 43 仙挂牌，公众部分获 80.42 倍超额认购，上市市值约 RM385.1 million。截至 9 月 30 日的九个月净利 RM26.14 million，营业额 RM270.67 million，第三季净利 RM14.37 million；手头订单约 RM313.37 million。

    SKACAP来源： The Star ↗
  3. 大市

    9 月工业生产按年增 6.3%，制造业增速加快

    统计局数据显示，9 月工业生产指数按年增长 6.3%，按月增 1.3%。制造业按年增 9.4%（8 月为 7.4%），电力增 10.7%，矿业则下滑 9.4%；制造业销售额达 RM191.1 billion。

    来源： The Star ↗
  4. 大市

    10 月棕油库存升至 362 万吨，出口按月大增

    大马棕油局数据显示，10 月底棕油库存按月增 3.15% 至 362 万吨，其中原棕油库存增 7.46% 至 232 万吨。原棕油产量增 1.74% 至 215 万吨，棕油出口按月激增 55.75% 至 170 万吨。

    来源： The Star ↗
  5. 业绩

    协成工业第三季净利跌至 RM14m，派息 2 仙

    饼干商协成工业（Hup Seng）第三季净利 RM14.03 million，低于去年同期的 RM15.47 million；营业额从 RM105.7 million 降至 RM98.7 million，国内销售减少 4%，出口则增 5%。九个月净利微增至 RM34.95 million，董事部建议派发第三次中期股息每股 2 仙。

    HUPSENG来源： The Star ↗
  6. 政策

    仲量联行：放贷趋严、利率或上调，明年房市面对挑战

    仲量联行（JLL）马来西亚预计，银行明年审批房贷将更为严格，加上隔夜政策利率可能上调推高借贷成本，依赖房贷的购屋者融资难度或增加，对住宅及整体房地产市场构成压力。

    来源： The Star ↗

KLCI falls 0.55% to 1,595.19; Wall Street slips on hot US inflation as SKA Capital debuts on ACE today

Wed, 11 Nov 202612Invest
FBM KLCI · Wed, 11 Nov 2026 close
1,595.19
Change
-8.88（-0.55%）

Hotter-than-expected US October inflation reinforced bets on higher-for-longer Fed rates and pushed US Treasury yields towards 5%, weighing on financial heavyweights and dragging the index below the 1,600 level, though gainers outnumbered losers on the broader market. Source: Bernama ↗

  1. Global

    Hotter-than-expected US October inflation sends Wall Street lower on Tuesday

    US October CPI rose 0.4% month on month and core prices 0.3%, both above forecasts, lifting the 10-year Treasury yield 13.8 basis points to 5.00% as markets bet on a Fed hike in December. The Dow fell 0.30% to 52,231.89, the S&P 500 0.38% and the Nasdaq 0.74%; Nvidia dropped about 6% intraday amid a lawsuit involving its CEO.

    Source: TheStreet ↗
  2. IPO

    SKA Capital debuts on the ACE Market today after RM26.1m nine-month profit

    Substation engineering firm SKA Capital lists at 43 sen a share, with its public portion 80.42 times oversubscribed and a market value of about RM385.1 million at listing. Net profit for the nine months to Sept 30 was RM26.14 million on revenue of RM270.67 million, including RM14.37 million in the third quarter; its order book is about RM313.37 million.

    SKACAPSource: The Star ↗
  3. Market

    September industrial output up 6.3% year on year as manufacturing accelerates

    The Statistics Department said the industrial production index rose 6.3% year on year in September and 1.3% month on month. Manufacturing grew 9.4% (August: 7.4%) and electricity 10.7%, while mining fell 9.4%; manufacturing sales reached RM191.1 billion.

    Source: The Star ↗
  4. Market

    October palm oil stocks rise to 3.62 million tonnes as exports jump

    MPOB data showed end-October palm oil stocks up 3.15% month on month at 3.62 million tonnes, with crude palm oil stocks up 7.46% at 2.32 million tonnes. CPO output rose 1.74% to 2.15 million tonnes, while palm oil exports surged 55.75% to 1.70 million tonnes.

    Source: The Star ↗
  5. Results

    Hup Seng's third-quarter profit slips to RM14m; 2 sen dividend declared

    Biscuit maker Hup Seng Industries posted third-quarter net profit of RM14.03 million, down from RM15.47 million a year earlier, as revenue fell to RM98.7 million from RM105.7 million on a 4% drop in domestic sales, partly offset by 5% export growth. Nine-month profit edged up to RM34.95 million, and the board recommended a third interim dividend of 2 sen a share.

    HUPSENGSource: The Star ↗
  6. Policy

    JLL: tighter lending and possible rate hike to challenge the property market next year

    JLL Malaysia expects banks to be more selective in approving home loans next year, while a possible rise in the Overnight Policy Rate would raise borrowing costs, making financing harder for buyers who depend on mortgages and weighing on residential and wider property demand.

    Source: The Star ↗
